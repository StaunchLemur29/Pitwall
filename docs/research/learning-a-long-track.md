# Learning a long track, and measuring progress sensibly

Research notes for Pitwall, focused on the Nürburgring Nordschleife. Gathered November 2026.

**About the sources.** This session's network blocked direct page fetches, so sources were read through search-engine extracts and abstracts, not full pages. Much of the Nordschleife advice comes from guides and experienced drivers, not peer-reviewed studies. That is noted wherever it applies.

## The short version

- Learn the Nordschleife in sections, by name, and stitch them together later.
- Learn the layout first, then the speed. Add pace a little at a time, with the most margin over blind crests.
- Judge progress by your typical clean section time and your spread, not by a single personal best.
- Plateaus are normal. When progress stalls, change what you practise, not how much.

## Why this track is different

The Nordschleife is about 20.8 km with 73 officially counted corners and more than 300 m of elevation change ([VLN/NLS](https://www.nuerburgring-langstrecken-serie.de/en/the-nurburgring); [Porsche](https://www.porsche.com/stories/experience/how-to-drive-the-nuerburgring-nordschleife/)). Other counts run past 150, because many bends merge into named complexes ([summary of counts](https://lapmeta.com/fr/track/variation/12)). Autosport sums it up: blind corners, steep crests and very little run-off, "not a track you can simply rock up to and master within a few laps" ([Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/general/news/how-to-tame-the-nordschleife-5109656/5109656/)).

The length matters for practice too. A mistake in one place means about 5–6 minutes of driving before you get another try at it ([Overtake forum](https://www.overtake.gg/threads/learning-nordschleife.130819/)). Over a session, that adds up to very few attempts per corner.

## Chunking into sections

Every guide says the same thing: break the lap into sections. Ross Bentley says a track like the Nordschleife "requires breaking the 20km track down into sections before attempting any serious learning" ([Winding Road](https://windingroad.com/?p=1047)). Driver61 suggests learning it in small chunks and watching their video guide one section at a time ([Driver61 Nordschleife guide](https://driver61.com/nurburgring-pdf/)). Bentley's own Nordschleife material is split into named sections, such as "Karussell to Brünnchen" ([Speed Secrets PDF](https://speedsecrets.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/8-Karussell-to-Brunnchen.pdf)). Other tips:

- Learn the German corner names and link each to a map and cockpit view. A name usually covers several corners, not one apex ([amenzwa Nordschleife notes](https://amenzwa.github.io/motorsport/GNF/Nordschleife)).
- Count corners to and from landmarks such as the banked Karussells ([GTPlanet](https://gtplanet.net/forum/threads/tips-for-mastering-nurburgring-nordschleife.369015)).
- Rehearse around six corners at a time ([Vicarious](https://magazines.vicariousmag.com/TeaserFall2025/18/)).

Research backs this up. In sequence learning, people naturally group moves into chunks, and with practice small chunks merge into longer ones ([Sakai et al. 2003, as summarised](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3372854)). Part-task training, practising a segment and then the whole, can help. Practising backwards ("backward chaining", final section first) transferred well in carrier-landing training. A review warns that part-task training often does no better than practising the whole task, so you still need to stitch sections together ([Wightman & Lintern 1985](https://doi.org/10.1177/001872088502700304); [Wightman 1983, DTIC](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA136670.pdf)).

## Video, onboards and the sim

Watch fast laps and guide videos one section at a time ([Driver61](https://driver61.com/nurburgring-pdf/); [GTPlanet](https://gtplanet.net/forum/threads/tips-for-mastering-nurburgring-nordschleife.369015)). Real-world drivers learn the Nordschleife in simulators before going there. Next Level Racing and Track Speed run a programme for it, though sims don't reproduce the bumps ([Next Level Racing](https://nextlevelracing.com/news/next-level-racing-partners-nurburgring-specialists-track-speed-gmbh/); [Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/general/news/how-to-tame-the-nordschleife-5109656/5109656/)). Look ahead. Racing drivers in a simulator looked further down the road than non-racers ([van Leeuwen et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29121090/)).

## Reference points

Give each corner five markers: braking, turn-in, slowest point, apex and exit ([NASA Speed News](https://nasaspeed.news/columns/driver-instruction/track-walks-the-what-where-why-and-how/)). In a sim, boards, kerbs, trees and buildings all work ([Asetek guide](https://www.asetek.com/simsports/guides/simple-tips-to-learn-a-new-track-fast-in-sim-racing/)). Bentley suggests aiming at where braking should *end*, because the start then gets easier to find ([Bentley](https://rossbentley.substack.com/p/speed-secrets-just-5-seconds)).

## Building speed, with margin on blind crests

- **Learn the track before you learn to go fast.** Don't attack every corner at first. Exit speed is where the big gains are ([GTPlanet](https://gtplanet.net/forum/threads/tips-for-mastering-nurburgring-nordschleife.369015)). Start with slow laps and build up ([Overtake](https://overtake.gg/threads/learning-nordschleife.130819/page-2)). A first lap is a hard country road, not a time attack ([Autosport](https://www.autosport.com/motorsport/news/131263/a-beginner-guide-to-the-nordschleife)).
- **Brake before or after a crest, never on it.** Still braking hard over a drop means landing in a skid ([FastCar](https://www.fastcar.co.uk/tuning-tech-guides/nurburgring-nordschleife-cornering-map-and-guide/)). Most corners are blind, and you are committed before you see the exit ([Vicarious](https://magazines.vicariousmag.com/TeaserFall2025/18/)).
- **"Drive what you can see."** That's tourist-day advice from regulars, and a forum view ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/racing-and-drivers-education-forum/221711-nurburgring-regulars-tips-advice-re-tourist-days.html)).
- **Don't freeze in the hard bit.** Many drivers go into survival mode between the two Karussells instead of driving the section properly ([search extract of a forum thread](https://forums.autosport.com/topic/216606-finally-learning-the-nordschleife)).
- **Clean beats fast.** "It is not about a fast lap, it is about a fast and clean lap" ([Autosport beginner's guide](https://www.autosport.com/general/news/the-nurburgring-nordschleife-a-beginners-guide-4997347/4997347/)).

None of these sources gives a number for "how much margin". It's a judgement call. The sensible version is to take blind crests a step under the pace of visible corners until the driver repeatedly hits the same marker there.

## Measuring improvement sensibly

**A personal best is a poor progress measure.** It's one extreme lap and it only ever goes down. A median of clean laps, plus their spread (standard deviation), says more. Count only clean laps, with no offs or incidents ([west-games lap calculator](https://west-games.com/lap-time-delta-calculator/)). A consistent driver has a median close to their best and a small spread ([consistency write-up](https://lap-website-dif.pages.dev/blog/consistency-over-raw-pace)). These are practitioner sources. I found no research that sets a target spread.

**Use sections, not whole laps.** A full Nordschleife lap blends dozens of corners, and one mistake hides a good lap everywhere else. Sim coaches split the lap into sections and go after the biggest gap first ([Coach Dave](https://coachdaveacademy.com/tutorials/learn-a-new-track-faster-using-coach-dave-delta/)). Section times also let partial laps count.

**Learning curves flatten, and that's normal.** Improvement is fast at first and then slows. Whether it follows a power law or an exponential curve is debated, and smooth group curves hide jumpy individual ones ([Power law of practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_law_of_practice); [Heathcote et al. / Snoddy revisited](https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/en/publications/snoddy-1926-revisited-time-scales-of-motor-learning-2/); [individual curves differ, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8686050)). For an experienced driver, expect small gains and long flat stretches.

**Plateaus come from autopilot.** Ericsson argues that experience alone predicts performance weakly. Progress comes from focused practice on specific weaknesses, with feedback ([Ericsson 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18778378/)). A secondary summary adds that practice without that focus leads to automaticity and "arrested development" ([ETSU primer](https://www.ETSU.edu/com/acadaffairs/documents/cpywrt2018_univcalsanfran_pearltheories_deliberatepractice.pdf)). So the fix for a plateau is to change what you practise, not to add more laps.

**Spread is a skill signal too.** Bandwidth-feedback studies found that learners became more consistent, not only more accurate ([Lee & Carnahan 1990](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/1313886)). A shrinking spread at a section is real progress, even when the best time stays put.

## What this means for Pitwall

Ranked, most useful first.

1. **Track progress per section with a rolling median and spread, not the PB.** For each section, show the median of the last N clean passes (say 10) and how wide they spread, with the PB as a faint marker. "Your usual through Hatzenbach dropped 0.3 s" is the honest version of "become better". *Partly exists:* per-corner "usual" and time to beat. *New:* a section-level rolling median and spread chart in the hub.
2. **Count every clean pass of a section, including partial laps and drill runs.** One spin shouldn't throw away the 15 good sections around it. *Partly exists* (drills, per-corner analysis). Check that section and corner stats come from passes, not completed laps.
3. **A Nordschleife section map built on the named sections.** Use the German names (Hatzenbach, Flugplatz, Adenauer Forst, Fuchsröhre, Bergwerk, Karussell, Brünnchen and so on) as the units for drills, stats and debriefs. *New for this track*, though drills on sections already exist.
4. **Drills on weak sections that never reset.** Teleport to the section start, run it, and keep a running median that carries across sessions and days. Once a section is learnt, mix two or three sections (see coaching.md on varied practice). Every so often, run a full lap to stitch the sections together. *Exists:* drills with retry and teleport. *New:* persistence across sessions, a mixed mode, and a "stitch" lap.
5. **Plateau detection that changes the practice, quietly.** When a section's median hasn't moved for a few sessions, the debrief suggests one different approach (a new reference point, a different brake marker, an exit-speed focus) rather than "more laps". Suggest, never nag. *New*, using the AI debrief.
6. **A crest-safety flag.** Mark known blind crests and compressions. Base the brake countdown and stretch targets there on margin, not raw optimal. Flag "braking on the crest" as a mistake pattern (from FastCar's rule). *New.* Note that the optimal run doesn't know about crests and bumps.
7. **Build speed in steps for a new car or track.** Start time-to-beat targets near the driver's usual and ratchet them up only once spread at that section is small. This is the "layout first, then speed" advice. *Partly exists* (the stretch target moves from usual towards best).
8. **Reference markers per corner.** Let the driver save their own brake marker ("the 100 board", "the tree on the left") so the live card names it instead of a distance. Link these to the fading cues idea in coaching.md. *New, optional.*
9. **Clean-lap rules.** Leave off-track and incident passes out of medians automatically, so the numbers stay honest. *Check* the current behaviour in the analysis code.
